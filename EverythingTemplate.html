Introduction

This showcase project demonstrates all available display options for your portfolio. From full-width videos to before/after sliders, image grids, and slideshow variants—this page serves as a complete reference for building compelling project presentations.

Display Option 1
Before/After Slider

The before/after slider allows visitors to compare two images by dragging a handle left or right. Perfect for showing transformations, AI enhancements, color grading comparisons, or design iterations. You can stack multiple sliders vertically for sequential comparisons.

Display Option 2
Full-Width Single Image

A simple full-width image display that bleeds edge-to-edge across the viewport. Ideal for hero images, final renders, or any standalone visual that deserves maximum impact and attention without distractions.

Display Option 3
Image Grid (Side-by-Side)

The image grid displays multiple images side-by-side in a responsive layout. On desktop, images appear in rows; on mobile, they stack vertically. Perfect for showing variations, multiple angles, or related designs in a clean, organized format.

Display Option 4
Full-Width Video

Full-width video players that automatically loop and can be controlled by visitors. On mobile, video controls are hidden initially and appear when tapped, creating a clean, cinematic presentation. Ideal for animations, motion graphics, or process videos.

Display Option 5
Fade Slideshow with Auto-Play

This slideshow fades between images with automatic progression (6 seconds per slide). Visitors can manually navigate using left/right arrows that appear on hover. The slideshow loops infinitely and pauses when you hover over it. Great for showing a series of related images or process steps.

Display Option 6
Sliding Slideshow with Auto-Bounce

This variant slides images horizontally (left to right) with smooth transitions. It auto-plays with a "bounce" effect—moving forward to the last image, then reversing back to the first, creating a seamless loop. Arrows are hidden at the ends. Use this for sequential narratives or progressive reveals where order matters.
